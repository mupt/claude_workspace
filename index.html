🦎 Lizards

Masters of survival, older than the dinosaurs

What Are Lizards?

Lizards are a widespread group of squamate reptiles, with over 7,000 known species distributed across every continent except Antarctica. They range from tiny geckos no bigger than a thumbnail to the fearsome Komodo dragon, which can reach 3 metres in length and take down a water buffalo.

Unlike snakes — their closest relatives — most lizards have four legs, visible ear openings, and movable eyelids. They are cold-blooded, relying on external heat sources to regulate their body temperature, which is why you'll so often find them basking on sun-warmed rocks.

Notable Species

🐉

Komodo Dragon

The world's largest lizard, native to a handful of Indonesian islands. It uses venom and bacteria-laden saliva to bring down prey far larger than itself.

🏜️

Thorny Devil

An Australian desert lizard covered in spines. It collects moisture from sand through capillary channels in its scales and channels it directly to its mouth.

🌿

Green Iguana

A large, arboreal lizard native to Central and South America. Despite its intimidating size, it is almost entirely herbivorous and an excellent swimmer.

🌈

Chameleon

Famous for changing colour — not primarily for camouflage, but to communicate mood and social status. Their eyes move independently, giving 360° vision.

🏃

Basilisk

Known as the "Jesus lizard" for its ability to run across water on its hind legs. It achieves this through rapid foot strikes that trap air pockets beneath the surface.

🏠

Common House Gecko

A familiar sight across the tropics, clinging to walls and ceilings at night. Their toe pads contain millions of microscopic hairs that exploit van der Waals forces.

Fascinating Facts

  • Some lizards can detach their tails to escape predators — and regrow them over time.
  • The blue-tongued skink flashes its vivid tongue to startle and deter threats.
  • Geckos can hear sounds, unlike most other lizards which rely primarily on vibration.
  • The Gila monster is one of only two venomous lizard species native to North America.
  • Some skink species give birth to live young and even have a primitive placenta.
  • The frilled-neck lizard erects a large frill around its neck to appear larger when threatened.
  • Komodo dragons can reproduce via parthenogenesis — females can produce offspring without a male.

Lizards & Their Habitat

Lizards have colonised nearly every terrestrial habitat on Earth — from the driest deserts to tropical rainforests, coastal dunes to high-altitude mountainsides. Their success lies in their adaptability: desert species have evolved to extract water from food alone, while rainforest species can be brilliantly coloured and highly arboreal.

Many species are under increasing pressure from habitat destruction, invasive predators, and climate change. Rising temperatures are particularly threatening, as they can skew sex ratios in species where egg incubation temperature determines sex.

🦎 The world is a better place with lizards in it.
